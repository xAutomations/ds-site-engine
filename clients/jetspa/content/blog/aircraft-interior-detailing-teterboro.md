  - "## How Does the Cabin Walkthrough Set the Scope?"
  - "The process starts with the pilot or ground crew. JetSpa inspects the cabin, notes stains, odors, visible wear, and damaged areas, and confirms the requested work in writing. This is the right time to identify a coffee spill beside a seat rail, a dull veneer panel, a lavatory issue, or a persistent odor that has returned after earlier cleaning. Loose items are removed before detailed work begins and replaced during the final pass."
  - "A walkthrough also protects the cabin from one-size-fits-all treatment. Aircraft interiors can contain leather, certified aviation fabrics, flame-resistant materials, wood veneer, plated trim, and sensitive controls within a compact space. The product and method need to match the surface. JetSpa documents that it uses OEM-approved chemistry rather than automotive substitutes, and confirms the scope, timeline, and quote before service."
  - "## How Is an Aircraft Cabin Vacuumed From Headliner to Floor?"
  - "Detailed vacuuming works from the upper cabin down to the floor. The scope extends beyond the visible center aisle to the seams and transitions where debris collects: around seat frames, along seat rails, beside cabinetry, and at floor edges. Working methodically matters because crumbs, dust, and grit left near moving seat components or pressed into carpet can remain visible even when the aisle looks clean."
  - "The vacuum stage also prepares the cabin for surface care. Removing loose contamination first keeps grit from being dragged across leather, veneer, or hard surfaces during later work. It gives the technician a clearer view of spots that need focused cleaning and shows whether the carpet needs routine vacuuming or deep extraction."
  - "## How Are Leather, Wood Veneer, and Cabin Surfaces Cleaned?"
  - "Leather is cleaned and conditioned with an OEM-approved conditioner. Conditioning supports the material rather than masking it with a glossy automotive dressing. Wood veneer receives polish and protection, while the cabin's other hard surfaces are cleaned according to their finish. Headliners and sidewalls are handled as distinct materials, not treated with the same chemical used on a tray table or armrest."
  - "This surface-by-surface approach is one of the main differences between aircraft cabin care and a generic interior cleaning. The wrong cleaner can alter a finish or damage an expensive component. JetSpa's documented policy is straightforward: if a product is not approved by the aircraft's original equipment manufacturer, it is not used. Every JetSpa technician also holds Aircraft Detailing Specialist certification from the Aircraft Detailing Association."
  - "The final surface pass focuses on presentation and high-touch points. Armrests, tray tables, and cabin controls are disinfected. Loose items are returned, and the completed work is documented before handback. The goal is an orderly cabin with clean transitions, properly treated materials, and no overlooked zone that becomes obvious as soon as a passenger sits down."
  - "## Are the Galley and Lavatory Part of a Full Interior Detail?"
  - "Yes. JetSpa includes both areas in a full Interior Detail. The galley scope covers the sink, countertops, coffee-brewer surfaces, and other accessible hard surfaces. The lavatory is detailed as part of the cabin, with deeper lavatory cleaning available when heavier use calls for additional work."
  - "These areas deserve specific attention because they can be the source of spills and persistent odors. Cleaning only the passenger seating area can leave the cause of a cabin problem untouched. During the walkthrough, the crew should point out any recent spill, recurring smell, or high-use area so it can be included in the written scope instead of discovered near the end of the service window."
  - "## When Does Carpet Extraction or Odor Removal Make Sense?"
  - "Carpet extraction is an add-on for embedded soil and residue that detailed vacuuming cannot lift. JetSpa uses hot-water extraction for carpets and cabin fabrics when ordered, and performs that deep work after the main surface work. It can be bundled with an interior detail for a fuller cabin refresh. Because aircraft size and cabin condition vary, the need and timeline are confirmed before work begins."
  - "Deep odor removal is also an add-on. JetSpa's documented process starts with source elimination through extraction and fabric treatment, then may use ozone or hydrogen peroxide fogging. That sequence is important. Adding a treatment without addressing the spilled food, contaminated carpet, or lavatory source underneath does not solve the underlying issue. Long-term smoke odor may require component-level intervention, so it should not be presented as a guaranteed quick fix."

  - "A full Interior Detail goes further. It includes leather conditioning, all-surface cleaning, seat-frame detailing, headliner care, and a detailed vacuum, plus the galley and lavatory scope. JetSpa lists 45 to 90 minutes as a typical range for a light jet, while midsize and larger cabins take longer. The actual duration depends on aircraft class and condition and is confirmed in the written quote. A trip clean should not be expected to correct embedded carpet contamination, a persistent odor, or accumulated wear."
